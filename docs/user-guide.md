# Study Buddy — User Guide

*For the student*

> **Note:** This is the version I ship to my own kid. The standing tasks, sports references, and team mentions are specific to him. When you set up Study Buddy for your student, rewrite this file to match their actual subjects, sports, and interests. Treat it as a template, not a generic guide.

---

## What Is This?

Every Sunday evening, a new set of tasks shows up in your TickTick list. These tasks are generated automatically from your school assignment calendar. They break down your upcoming work into specific steps, tell you what to do and when to do it, and ask you a quick question at the end of each one.

You don't need to set anything up. You don't need to plan your week. Just open TickTick and follow what's there.

---

## What You'll See

### Assignment tasks

When you have a test, essay, quiz, project, or homework due, tasks appear before the deadline — not just on the due date. For example:

**Test due Thursday** might generate:
- Monday evening: *Study: full unit review — find your weak spots*
- Tuesday evening: *Study: focused review of weak areas*
- Thursday morning: *Day-of review: 15-minute final scan*

**Essay due Friday** might generate:
- Monday: *Brainstorm and outline*
- Tuesday: *Gather evidence*
- Wednesday: *First draft*
- Thursday: *Revise and strengthen*
- Friday morning: *Final proofread + submit*

The idea is that by the time the deadline arrives, you're not starting from zero — you've already done the work in pieces.

### Standing tasks

Three recurring tasks appear every week regardless of what's assigned:

- **🇮🇱 Hebrew vocab review** (10 min) — Mon/Tue/Wed/Thu and Sunday evenings
- **📐 Algebra: pre-class review** (10 min) — Mon/Tue/Wed/Thu evenings
- **🔁 Weekly debrief** (10 min) — Friday afternoon

These are short on purpose. The point is consistency, not cramming.

### Schedule-aware timing

Tasks are given specific times, not just dates. A few things affect those times:

- **Lacrosse game days:** Tasks that evening are either moved earlier or skipped entirely — game days are long and you need the evening.
- **Lacrosse practice days:** Tasks are scheduled after practice ends, with at least an hour buffer.
- **Bruins game nights:** If there's a home or away game starting at or before 7 PM, tasks are shifted 90 minutes earlier so you can catch the puck drop.
- **Fridays:** School gets out early, so tasks are scheduled for the afternoon.

You can always move tasks around in TickTick if the time doesn't work for you. The times are a starting point, not a rule.

## How to Use a Task

Each task has two parts when you open it:

**1. What to do** — specific steps for that task type. Read them before you start. They're there because vague instructions lead to vague results.

**2. The debrief** — at the bottom of every task, there's a short prompt and a score line:

```
🔔 DEBRIEF
[A question about how it went]
Score: __/5
Notes:
```

Fill in the score and a note when you're done. This takes 30 seconds and it matters — see below.

---

## The Score System

Rate yourself **1–5** when you complete each task:

| Score | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| 5 | Solid. I got this. |
| 4 | Pretty good, minor gaps. |
| 3 | Shaky. I got through it but I'm not confident. |
| 2 | Struggled. I need help. |
| 1 | Lost. I don't know what I'm doing. |

**If you score a 3 or below**, a follow-up task is automatically created: *📅 Book office hours: [Teacher Name]*. That task will appear in your list with a due date of the coming Friday. Book the time. It takes 10 minutes with your teacher and it prevents the confusion from compounding.

---

## Project Tasks

Projects are different because teachers usually post the start and end date without listing intermediate milestones. When a project appears in your list, the first task you'll see is:

**🗺️ [Subject] project: define milestones**

This task has a checklist inside it with placeholder steps. **Your first job is to rewrite those placeholders** to match what the actual project requires. Read the assignment sheet, figure out what the real phases are, and update the checklist. Set realistic dates for each milestone so the work is distributed across the available time.

If you're not sure how to break it down, that's exactly the right time to talk to your teacher — before you've started, not after you're stuck.

---

## Weekly Debrief

Every Friday at 3:30 PM, there's a 10-minute debrief task. It asks three questions:

1. What did I actually finish this week vs. what did I skip or rush?
2. Where did I score 3 or below — and did I follow up?
3. What's one thing I'd do differently next week?

Write two or three sentences. Not an essay. The point is to make the reflection real so it actually changes what you do next week.

---

## What Doesn't Show Up (And Why)

A few things are intentionally excluded:

- **Class schedule blocks** (A Block, B Block, etc.) are filtered out — those aren't tasks, they're just appointments.
- **School events** (Minyan, Advisory, Lunch, Community Time) are filtered out.
- **Saturdays** are completely empty — that day is yours.
- **No-school days** (holidays, breaks, Patriots Day) are skipped automatically.

If an assignment doesn't appear in your list, it's possible the teacher hasn't posted it to the school calendar yet, or it was entered with a description that didn't match any known format. Check the school portal directly if something seems missing.

## Tips

**Don't move tasks to "later" indefinitely.** If you're pushing a task three days in a row, that's a signal — either the time estimate is off, or you're avoiding something you don't understand. Score it low and book office hours.

**Do the standing tasks even when they feel pointless.** Ten minutes of Hebrew vocab or Algebra review every day beats two hours the night before. The whole system is built on this.

**Use the Notes field.** Writing "I kept mixing up vocab from Unit 3" or "I don't understand how to set up the equation" takes 10 seconds and is exactly what your teacher needs to hear when you book office hours.

**The debrief score is for you, not for your parents.** Be honest. A score of 2 that leads to a teacher conversation is better than a score of 5 that covers up a real gap.
